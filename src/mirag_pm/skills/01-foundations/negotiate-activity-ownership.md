## When

Any question of the form "whose job is this". The corpus has measured evidence that
practitioners disagree, which makes assertion the wrong move.

## Method

1. **Refuse the title-based inference.** `pm-and-ux-collaboration.md`: who owns a given
   activity *"is not derivable from the job title"*, and ownership of discovery, ideation,
   early design and voice-of-customer *"is ambiguous by default and must be established
   explicitly per organization and per project, ideally before work begins."*
2. **Surface the measured disagreement** for the activity in question. The survey (n=372)
   gives the splits: discovery was the biggest disagreement; only 46% of PMs considered user
   testing a UX activity; ideation was assigned to UX by more than three quarters of UX
   respondents and about a third of PMs; on information architecture, 50% of PMs said
   development and 75% of UXers said UX; voice-of-the-customer was the most fragmented at 9%
   versus 54%.
3. **Check the method dependency before drawing the PM/PO line.** Outside Scrum, the product
   manager may end up doing the prioritisation the product owner would otherwise do.
4. **Write the agreement down**, activity by activity, and name what stayed unresolved.

## Output

An ownership table for the activities in play, with the contested ones marked as contested.

## What this cannot claim

That there is a correct answer. The corpus's contribution here is evidence that the question
is genuinely open, not a resolution of it.
