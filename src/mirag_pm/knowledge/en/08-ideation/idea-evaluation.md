# Evaluating and Converging on Ideas

## Why this is a separate activity

Generating ideas and choosing among them are **different activities that must not be run
simultaneously** — see [brainstorming.md](brainstorming.md). But separating them means the
convergence step needs its own method, and this is where ideation processes most often stop
short.

[S136] names it as a failure mode: sessions that end with "**scattered sticky notes, half-baked
ideas**" and "**leaving without clear ownership**." Its framing: "Brainstorming isn't about
generating more ideas. It's about generating better ideas faster, **then actually building
them.**"

[S010] builds convergence into the 6-3-5 procedure as a mandatory final step: "a **preliminary
screening** of the ideas that have been gathered, where **exact duplicates are deleted**, and a
team evaluation takes place... to select **1 to 3 ideas** the group can focus on."

> **The 108 ideas a 6-3-5 session produces are not a result.** One to three chosen ideas are.

## Convergence techniques named in the corpus

| Technique | Source and description |
|---|---|
| **Affinity mapping** | [S136]: silently group related items, "**let natural clusters emerge without predefined categories**," name each cluster, identify relationships, "determine which clusters represent priority areas." See [brainstorming.md](brainstorming.md) |
| **Nominal Group Technique (NGT)** | [S010] recommends it for 6-3-5 evaluation; [S125] and [S100] list it as a risk-identification technique. **The corpus never explains how NGT works** — recorded as a gap |
| **Prioritization matrix** | [S010] names it as an alternative to NGT for selecting from a brainwriting session. See [../05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](../05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md) |
| **Group voting** | [S001]: "**Vote as a group** on the top solutions to each question" |
| **Duplicate removal** | [S010]: "exact duplicates are deleted" — the cheapest first pass |

> For anything beyond a single session's output, the prioritization frameworks in
> [../05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](../05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md) are
> the better instrument. The techniques above are for **reducing a session's raw output to a
> shortlist**; RICE, weighted scoring and their relatives are for **allocating capacity across
> shortlisted candidates.**

## Evaluating ideas systematically

For evaluation beyond a workshop, [S030] provides a screening process. Treated in full in
[../02-discovery/opportunity-assessment.md](../02-discovery/opportunity-assessment.md); summarised
here because it is the natural next step after ideation.

### Ideas versus opportunities
[S030] separates two things that ideation produces:
- **Ideas** — "potential product concepts or designs... entirely new products, or modifications or
  improvements to existing products." Sources: "**internal brainstorming sessions**, customer
  feedback, market research, and technological advancements."
- **Opportunities** — "potential market opportunities that a product could target... a new market
  segment, a new geographic market, or a new use case."

### The two-stage funnel
[S030]:

| | **Initial screening** | **Detailed analysis** | **Decision making** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Objective** | "Assess initial viability" | "Gather in-depth insights" | "Choose pursuit strategy" |
| **Activities** | SWOT or PESTEL analysis | "Market research, competitor analysis, financial modeling, prototype testing" | "Evaluate data, decide" |
| **Benefit** | "**Filters non-viable options**" | "Informs strategic choices" | "Guides resource allocation" |

**The purpose of the first gate** [S030]: "to **quickly weed out any ideas or opportunities that are
clearly not viable, so that resources can be focused on more promising options.**"

### The three evaluation factors
[S030] applies the same three throughout:

| Factor | Role | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Market demand** | "Gauge customer need" | "Market size, trends" |
| **Competition** | "Assess market rivalry" | "Competitor products" |
| **Financial feasibility** | "Evaluate cost viability" | "Cost, profit potential" |

> These three are necessary but **not sufficient for product work** — they omit technical
> feasibility, strategic fit and risk, all of which [S081] includes. See
> [../02-discovery/opportunity-assessment.md](../02-discovery/opportunity-assessment.md) for the
> fuller six-step assessment.

## The quality test for an idea

[S009] offers a compact three-part test: an idea is worth pursuing if it is **"relevant,
differentiated, and worth paying for."**

> Simple, but it maps onto the three things the rest of this knowledge base treats seriously:
> *relevant* → a real user need
> ([../02-discovery/product-discovery.md](../02-discovery/product-discovery.md));
> *differentiated* → positioning ([../04-strategy/positioning.md](../04-strategy/positioning.md));
> *worth paying for* → value ([../04-strategy/product-value.md](../04-strategy/product-value.md)).

## From ideas to action

[S136] identifies the transition as where most sessions fail — "**zero structure for moving from
ideas to decisions**" — and names what is needed: **validation strategies, prioritization
frameworks, and clear ownership.**

The path this knowledge base supports:

```
Ideation session output (many ideas)
        ↓  duplicate removal, affinity mapping, voting/NGT
Shortlist (1–3 per session)
        ↓  initial screening: demand / competition / feasibility          [S030]
Viable candidates
        ↓  opportunity assessment for big bets                            [S081]
        ↓  prioritization framework for comparable candidates             [S066], [S031]
Prioritized candidates
        ↓  assumption mapping → validate the riskiest                     [S087], [S028]
Validated opportunities
        ↓
Roadmap                                                                   [S119]
```

> Note the branch: [S081] is explicit that **a full opportunity assessment is not always
> warranted** — for incremental work within a validated niche, go straight to a prioritization
> framework. Depth of evaluation should scale with the size and reversibility of the bet.

## Limitations

- **No source in the corpus is about idea evaluation as such.** This document assembles fragments
  from ideation, glossary and opportunity-assessment sources.
- **Nominal Group Technique is recommended twice and explained never.** This is the most
  conspicuous gap in this document.
- **Dot voting, impact/effort grids at workshop scale, and idea-scoring rubrics** are absent.
- **[S030] is a vendor glossary** that is repetitive and offers no thresholds — it says what to
  evaluate but never what result should trigger a *no*.
- **No treatment of how to preserve rejected ideas** or revisit them when context changes.
- **No treatment of who decides.** Every source describes group techniques; none addresses what
  happens when the group's shortlist conflicts with a decision-maker's preference.
