# Brainstorming — Techniques and When to Use Them

## The governing principle

Across every source in this domain, one rule recurs: **separate generation from evaluation.**

[S136]: "Separating generation from evaluation **produces both more ideas and better ones.**"
[S136] names mixing the two as a session-killing mistake.

[S010]'s 6-3-5 procedure makes the same separation structural — idea generation happens in
silence, and screening is a distinct final step. See [brainwriting.md](brainwriting.md).

> **This is the single most transferable idea in the ideation material.** Divergence and
> convergence are different cognitive activities; running them simultaneously suppresses both. It
> is the same logic as the Double Diamond in
> [../02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](../02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md).

## When to brainstorm — and when not to

[S136] is the corpus's only source that treats this conditionally, and it is the most useful
content on the page:

### Use brainstorming when you need to…
| Situation | [S136] |
|---|---|
| **Identify and frame problems worth solving** | "Before jumping to solutions, teams need to understand what they're actually trying to fix. Brainstorming helps **surface the real problem beneath surface-level symptoms**" |
| **Generate multiple solution approaches** | "When facing complex challenges with no obvious answer, brainstorming reveals diverse approaches **you wouldn't discover alone**" |
| **Make decisions with incomplete information** | "Brainstorming helps **evaluate trade-offs, surface assumptions, and align on direction despite uncertainty**" |
| **Align cross-functional teams on direction** | "When engineering, product, design, and marketing need to move together, collaborative ideation **builds shared understanding faster than email threads**" |
| **Break through creative blocks** | "When teams feel stuck repeating the same approaches" |

### Skip brainstorming when you…
| Situation | [S136] |
|---|---|
| **Already know the solution** | "If one clear path exists, validate it and move forward. **Don't waste time generating alternatives to check a process box**" |
| **Need deep expertise on a technical problem** | "**Some challenges require specialized knowledge, not diverse perspectives. Let the experts solve it**" |
| **Face time-critical execution decisions** | "**When the building is burning, make the call.** Brainstorm the prevention plan later" |
| **Haven't done basic research** | "**Brainstorming without context produces shallow ideas.** Talk to users, review data, understand the landscape first — then ideate" |

> [S136]'s framing: "**The best teams recognize brainstorming as a tool, not a ritual.**"
>
> The fourth *skip* condition matters most for product work and aligns with [S028]'s placement of
> ideation **at the end of discovery**, not the beginning. See
> [../02-discovery/product-discovery.md](../02-discovery/product-discovery.md).

## The techniques

### Mind mapping
*Treated in full in [mind-mapping.md](mind-mapping.md).*

[S136] — **when to use:** "Early-stage problem exploration when you need to understand how
different aspects of a challenge relate to each other." **Why it works:** "Mind maps match how
teams actually think — **associatively rather than linearly.**"

### Rapid ideation
**What it is** [S136]: "uses **time constraints to push past obvious solutions** and generate
volume before evaluating quality."

**When** [S136]: "When your team **keeps circling back to the same safe ideas**, or when you need to
fill the pipeline with options fast."

**How** [S136]: set a specific challenge → "give each participant **5-10 minutes** to generate as
many ideas as possible **individually**" → group similar concepts and identify patterns → select
the most promising directions.

**Why it works** [S136]: "**Time pressure short-circuits the internal editor that kills ideas before
you voice them.**"

[S023] adds: "The goal is to capture **high-level concepts without dampening creativity by thinking
too long about any one idea.**"

### Reverse brainstorming
**What it is** [S136]: "instead of asking '**how do we solve this?**' you ask '**how could we make
this worse?**' Then invert those ideas into solutions."

**How** [S136]: define the problem → reverse it ("How could we guarantee this fails?") → generate
ideas for causing the opposite outcome → **flip each negative idea into a potential solution** →
evaluate which inverted solutions address the actual challenge.

**Why it works** [S136]: "**It's easier to spot what could go wrong than what could go right.**
This technique reveals **hidden risks** and unconventional solutions your team wouldn't reach
through direct ideation."

[S023] agrees: "It is usually easier to spot problems than it is to find solutions... useful for
solving complex product problems like a major UX overhaul."

**A product-specific application** [S136]: "'**How could we make users abandon this feature?**'
quickly surfaces friction points your positive framing missed."

> Reverse brainstorming doubles as a **risk identification technique** — it surfaces failure modes,
> not just ideas. Compare the pre-mortem concept [S136] lists among its mental models, and
> [../10-risk/risk-identification.md](../10-risk/risk-identification.md).

### Starbursting
**What it is** [S136]: "generates **questions rather than answers**, helping teams thoroughly
explore a problem, idea, or opportunity **before committing to solutions.**"

**How** [S136]: place the idea in the centre of a star diagram → create **six points: Who, What,
Where, When, Why, How** → generate as many questions as possible for each → group related
questions → "**prioritize which questions must be answered before moving forward.**"

**Why it works** [S136]: "**Teams often jump to solutions before understanding the problem.**"

**Application** [S136]: "Use starbursting **before building features. 'Who needs this?' and 'Why
now?' reveal whether you're solving the right problem.**"

> This is effectively a structured way to generate **research questions** — the same output as
> [S028]'s research-question-generation workshop. See
> [../02-discovery/hypotheses-and-assumptions.md](../02-discovery/hypotheses-and-assumptions.md).

### Affinity mapping / affinity diagram
**What it is** [S136]: "helps teams **organize large volumes of ideas, feedback, or data into
meaningful categories that reveal patterns and priorities.**"

**When** [S136]: "After research, ideation, or discussion generates **more information than your
team can process linearly.** Essential for making sense of user research, feature requests, or
brainstorming output."

**How** [S136]: gather all items → "have team members **silently** group related items together" →
"**let natural clusters emerge without predefined categories**" → name each cluster → identify
relationships between clusters → determine priority areas.

**Why it works** [S136]: "**Pattern recognition beats predetermined categories.** Teams discover the
actual themes in their data rather than forcing information into expected buckets."

> This is a **convergence** technique, not a generation one. [S028] names affinity diagramming as
> the standard synthesis activity after exploratory research; [S125] and [S100] both list it as a
> risk-identification technique. It is the most cross-cutting method in the corpus.

### 5 Whys
[S023]: "designed to help you **dig into problems and determine the root cause.** You first define
the problem and then ask the group why it occurred. **Continue to ask why four more times** until
you uncover deeper [causes]."

[S016] names 5 Whys among risk analysis methods.

### Bad idea
[S023]: "This unconventional technique encourages you to think of **as many bad ideas as possible.**
As a prompt, you might ask: '**What is the worst solution you can imagine?**' Exploring absurd
ways..."

> A close relative of reverse brainstorming, aimed at lowering the stakes of contributing rather
> than at surfacing risk.

### Round robin
[S023] and [S001]: structured turn-taking so that every participant contributes in sequence.
[S001]: "**Round-robin brainstorming.**" *(The corpus's descriptions are thin.)*

### Question brainstorming
[S001] — generate **questions** rather than answers, then "**start answering questions the group
finds most important**":
1. "Each individual (or small group) takes 10-15 minutes to write as [many questions as possible]"
2. "After 10-15 minutes, the group gets back together to share"
3. "Start answering questions the group finds most important"

> Closely related to starbursting; the difference is that starbursting structures the questions
> along six dimensions.

### Hybrid brainstorming
[S001] describes combining individual and group work:
1. "Choose a set of **top questions (3-5)** before your group session"
2. "Each participant begins brainstorming in their **own dedicated** [space]"
3. "**Collect ideas in a shared digital space** to create a single [set]"
4. "**Vote as a group** on the top solutions to each question"

> The individual-then-group pattern is what [S129] and [S010] argue for structurally. See
> [brainwriting.md](brainwriting.md).

### Other techniques named
[S023] lists, without substantial description: **rolestorming, step ladder, SWOT**.
[S001] lists: **starbursting, crazy 8s**. [S136] additionally names mental models —
"**first principles thinking, second-order thinking, inversion, opportunity cost, and pre-mortem
analysis**" — none of which it develops.

> **Second-order thinking** is developed properly by [S106] in
> [../04-strategy/strategic-thinking.md](../04-strategy/strategic-thinking.md).
> **Pre-mortem** is not covered anywhere in the corpus — recorded as a gap.

## Why brainstorming matters for product teams

[S136] gives two arguments:

- **Alignment through participation:** "Teams that ideate together align faster. When everyone
  contributes to the solution, **you skip the 'I wasn't consulted' friction that slows execution.
  People support what they help create.**"
- **Compressed discovery:** "Instead of weeks of back-and-forth, you **map the problem space,
  generate options, and converge on direction in days.**"

[S136]'s closing claim: "**Brainstorming isn't about generating more ideas. It's about generating
better ideas faster, then actually building them.**"

## Common failure modes

[S136] names four:
1. **Mixing generation with evaluation**
2. **Letting loud voices dominate** — the problem [brainwriting.md](brainwriting.md) is designed to solve
3. **Leaving without clear ownership**
4. **Starting without a clear problem or the right people** — "**Most brainstorming sessions fail
   before they start. No clear problem, wrong people in the room, and zero structure for moving from
   ideas to decisions.**"

[S001] lists three properties of an effective session: **productivity, inclusivity, structure.**

## Limitations

- **All four sources are collaboration-software vendors** (Miro, Mural, Aha!) or their content
  marketing. Technique descriptions are interleaved with tool promotion, which has been excluded.
- **No evidence.** None of the productivity or quality claims is measured. [S010] is the only
  source in this domain with academic citations, and it covers only 6-3-5 — see
  [brainwriting.md](brainwriting.md).
- **The well-documented critique of group brainstorming is absent.** [S129] gestures at it ("fraught
  with problems... fear of evaluation... the serial nature of the process") but the corpus contains
  no source engaging with the research literature on brainstorming's effectiveness relative to
  individual ideation.
- **Facilitation is barely covered.** [S136] has a section on running sessions whose detail did not
  fully extract.
- **Several techniques are named only** (rolestorming, step ladder, crazy 8s, pre-mortem).
- **[S136] is dated February 2026** and includes AI-assisted ideation guidance that is
  tool-specific and time-sensitive; it has been excluded. See
  [../99-reference/outdated-information.md](../99-reference/outdated-information.md).
