# SCAMPER

## What it is

[S018]: "SCAMPER was first introduced by **Bob Eberle** to address **targeted questions that help
solve problems or ignite creativity during brainstorming meetings.** The name SCAMPER is an acronym
for seven techniques: **(S) substitute, (C) combine, (A) adapt, (M) modify, (P) put to another use,
(E) eliminate and (R) reverse.**"

[S136]: "SCAMPER provides **seven specific lenses for transforming existing ideas, products, or
processes.**"

### The underlying premise
[S018]: "The SCAMPER technique is based very simply on the idea that **what is new is actually a
modification of existing old things around us.**"

> This premise is also its **boundary condition**. SCAMPER operates on something that already
> exists. It is a technique for *transformation*, not for originating from nothing. That determines
> when it applies.

## Origin

[S101] gives an unusually detailed and well-specified account:

> "The foundation of SCAMPER traces back to **Alex Faickney Osborn**, the advertising executive who
> pioneered the concept of brainstorming. In **1953, Osborn published his groundbreaking book
> *Applied Imagination***, which contained an early version of what would later become SCAMPER."

**Osborn's original eight prompts** [S101]: *Adapt · Modify · Magnify · Minify · Substitute ·
Rearrange · Reverse · Combine*

> "Interestingly, **the SCAMPER acronym itself wasn't created until two decades later. Bob Eberle,
> an educational administrator from Edwardsville, Illinois, reorganized and refined Osborn's
> questions into the SCAMPER mnemonic** we know today. As a longtime leader at the **Creative
> Problem-Solving Institute (CPSI)**, Eberle was passionate about integrating creativity principles
> into traditional education... **he wanted to create a tool that would help children tap into their
> natural creativity.**"

> **Two things worth noting.** First, [S018] credits only Eberle; [S101] gives the fuller lineage
> back to Osborn. The accounts are compatible — Eberle reorganised Osborn's prompts — but [S018]
> alone would leave the impression that SCAMPER originated with Eberle.
>
> Second, **SCAMPER was designed as an educational tool for children**, later adopted by
> business. That is not a criticism, but it explains its simplicity and its mnemonic form.
>
> **Verification status: UNVERIFIED.** Neither *Applied Imagination* nor Eberle's work is in the
> corpus. The 1953 date and the eight original prompts come from a single vendor article.

## The seven lenses

Combining [S018]'s question sets with [S136]'s prompts:

| Lens | What it asks | Example questions |
|---|---|---|
| **S — Substitute** | "The parts of the product, service, or solution that **can be replaced with another**" [S018] | "What part of the process can be substituted **without affecting the whole project**?" · "Who or what can be substituted?" · "What can be replaced with **better alternatives**?" · "Can the project **time or place** be replaced?" · "Where else could you sell the product?" [S018] · "Materials, components, people, processes" [S136] |
| **C — Combine** | "What could we **merge**?" | "Features, products, steps, teams" [S136] |
| **A — Adapt** | "What could we **copy or adapt from elsewhere**?" | "Other industries, use cases, competitors" [S136] |
| **M — Modify** (also *minify or magnify*) | "What could we **change**?" | "Size, shape, format, frequency" [S136]. [S018] preserves Osborn's fuller form: **modify, minify or magnify** |
| **P — Put to another use** | "**How else could we use this?**" | "New audiences, contexts, purposes" [S136] |
| **E — Eliminate** (also *elaborate*) | "What could we **remove**?" | "Features, steps, **assumptions, constraints**" [S136] |
| **R — Reverse** | "What could we **flip or rearrange**?" | "Sequence, roles, relationships" [S136] |

> Note [S018]'s expanded labels — **"Modify, minify or magnify"** and **"Eliminate or elaborate"**.
> These preserve more of Osborn's original prompts than the compressed acronym does, and they
> double the number of moves available under those two letters. *Minify* (what if it were much
> smaller/simpler/cheaper?) is a distinct and productive question that "modify" alone does not
> prompt.
>
> Similarly, [S136]'s inclusion of **"assumptions, constraints"** under *Eliminate* is a
> significant extension: applied to constraints rather than features, *Eliminate* becomes a
> strategic question — *what if this limit weren't real?*

## Two rules for running it

[S018] states two operating principles:

### 1. No required sequence
"There is **no sequential flow to follow** while moving from each of the seven thinking techniques.
Unlike Disney's creative strategy method, **SCAMPER facilitators can move between different
techniques without being restricted to a specific flow.**"

### 2. Force-fitting
"**The principle of force-fitting should be adapted during the thinking sessions.** For example,
**any response to the SCAMPER technique is welcomed, no matter how non-logical it is.**"

> Force-fitting is what makes the technique work: you answer the prompt even when it seems
> inapplicable, because the strain of doing so is where non-obvious ideas come from. Filtering
> during generation defeats it — the same divergence/convergence separation as in
> [brainstorming.md](brainstorming.md).

### A third rule, from practice
[S136] adds a facilitation recommendation that conflicts mildly with exhaustive application:

> "**Focus on one or two SCAMPER lenses per session rather than trying to apply all seven. Going
> deep on 'eliminate' or 'reverse' produces better ideas than shallow passes through every
> option.**"

> This is a real tension. [S018] presents SCAMPER as a systematic sweep of all seven; [S136] argues
> depth beats coverage. Both are defensible; the choice depends on session length and whether the
> goal is breadth of options or a strong idea in one direction.

## Where it fits in a process

[S018] places it precisely within the Double Diamond:

> "In the Double Diamond Design Thinking, we go through four stages: **Discover, Define, Develop,
> and Deliver. The SCAMPER technique fits in the third stage, Develop.** Once we define the problem
> that we need to solve in the second stage, we can use the seven ways of thinking to try to
> investigate the different solutions."

> This is consistent with [S028]'s placement of ideation at the **end** of discovery, after the
> problem is framed. See
> [../02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](../02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md).

[S018] adds: "In other design thinking models, such as IDEO or IBM design thinking, the method can
be applied in a similar phase when we define the problem and enter the **prototyping stage.**"

**When to use it** [S136]: "When **improving existing products, iterating on current solutions, or
adapting successful approaches from other contexts.**"

## Why it works

[S018] gives a cognitive rationale:

> "**We usually tend to use existing mental models or existing solutions pathways that prevent us
> from seeing other innovative directions.** By changing how we see problems using the SCAMPER or
> other methods, such as Reversed Brainstorming, **we stimulate our neuron connections to build new
> connections that lead to new solutions.**"

> The neurological language is rhetorical, not evidential. The defensible core of the claim is
> that **structured prompts break default framing** — which [S136] states more soberly: "SCAMPER
> **forces systematic exploration rather than random ideation.** The structured prompts help teams
> discover improvements they'd miss through open-ended brainstorming."

## Related techniques named alongside it

[S018] situates SCAMPER among: "**reversed brainstorming, Hurson's thinking model, the six thinking
hats and Lego Serious Play**," describing SCAMPER as "one of the **easiest and most direct**"
of these.

> None of the other three is covered in the corpus. [S010] separately lists **Edward de Bono**
> (originator of Six Thinking Hats), **Synectics** and **TRIZ** as related methods — also
> uncovered. Recorded in [RESEARCH_BACKLOG.md](../RESEARCH_BACKLOG.md).

## Limitations

- **No evidence of effectiveness.** No source measures whether SCAMPER produces better ideas than
  unstructured ideation.
- **It cannot originate.** By its own premise, SCAMPER modifies what exists. For genuinely new
  problem spaces it has no input to operate on.
- **Attribution is second-hand** and the two sources differ in completeness (Eberle only vs
  Osborn→Eberle).
- **The seven-vs-two-lens tension is unresolved** (see above).
- **[S018] is an Interaction Design Foundation article** (published 2015, modified Aug 2026) with a
  template CTA; **[S101] is a vendor guide** whose historical section is its main contribution.
  Both are separable from their promotions.
- The "Bibliography" section of [S018] did not extract, so its sources cannot be checked.
