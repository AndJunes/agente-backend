# GLOSSARY

Terms as **this knowledge base's sources define them**, with the document that treats each in full.

> ## Rules applied to this glossary
>
> 1. **Every definition is sourced.** Definitions are quoted or closely paraphrased from a corpus
>    source, with its ID.
> 2. **Terms used but never defined by any source are listed as such** in
>    [§ Undefined terms](#undefined-terms). They are **not** defined from general knowledge.
> 3. **`[E]` marks a definition from Phase-2 external research**, not from the corpus. The citation
>    prefix tells you which register to resolve it in — `[Snnn]` corpus, `[Ennn]` external.
> 4. **Where sources disagree, both definitions are given** and marked ⚠.
> 5. A term's presence here does **not** mean the knowledge base can answer questions about it —
>    check the linked document.

## A

**Acceptance criteria** — "Details that help the team understand whether a story has been completed
correctly." May take the form of "examples, rules, tests, or brief notes." [S127] →
[06-requirements/acceptance-criteria.md](06-requirements/acceptance-criteria.md)

**Affinity mapping / affinity diagram** — Grouping ideas or data into clusters that "emerge without
predefined categories," then naming each cluster. [S136] →
[08-ideation/brainstorming.md](08-ideation/brainstorming.md)

**Agile Manifesto** — A 68-word document produced at Snowbird, Utah in early 2001 by 17 signatories,
stating four values. [S022] → [07-execution/agile-manifesto.md](07-execution/agile-manifesto.md)

**AARRR (Pirate Metrics)** — Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue. Attributed to
Dave McClure, 2007. [S021] → [09-metrics/pirate-metrics.md](09-metrics/pirate-metrics.md)

**Assumption** — "Anything you expect to be in place (yet isn't guaranteed)." [S084] →
[10-risk/assumptions-and-constraints.md](10-risk/assumptions-and-constraints.md)

**Assumption mapping** — Plotting assumptions "by importance and uncertainty. Assumptions that are
both critical and uncertain become top priorities for validation." [S087] →
[02-discovery/hypotheses-and-assumptions.md](02-discovery/hypotheses-and-assumptions.md)

## B

**Bottleneck / blocker** — [E] Distinct problems. A **bottleneck** is where flow is constrained by
one sub-process; a **blocker** is a dependency on another service. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Backlog (product)** — "A prioritized inventory of features, defects, or technical work that has
yet to be worked on." [S032] → [05-planning/backlog-management.md](05-planning/backlog-management.md)

**Backlog refinement** — Where "vague ideas become clearer stories, large stories are split, risks
surface, and acceptance criteria emerge." [S127] →
[05-planning/backlog-management.md](05-planning/backlog-management.md)

**Brainwriting** — "An ideation technique in which participants write their ideas in silence instead
of speaking aloud." [S137] → [08-ideation/brainwriting.md](08-ideation/brainwriting.md)

**BRD (Business Requirements Document)** — Addresses "the broader business needs and objectives
driving the product's development." Audience: executives and decision-makers. [S085] →
[06-requirements/prd.md](06-requirements/prd.md)

## C

**Cadence** — [E] "A review or meeting providing feedback from one or more services"; also the time
period between reviews. Kanban defines **seven**. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Capability eval** — [E] An evaluation aimed at difficult tasks, asking what an agent does well.
**Should start with a low pass rate**; graduates into the regression suite once saturated. [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**Classes of service** — [E] Different policies applied to different work types, derived from four
cost-of-delay archetypes: **expedite, fixed date, standard, intangible**. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Commitment point / delivery point** — [E] The boundaries of a kanban system. Work crosses the
commitment point when it enters; **Lead Time is measured between the two**. Without both defined,
a board is a flow system, not a kanban system. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Constitution (spec-kit)** — [E] Project principles — code quality, testing, maintainability —
established **once per project** and applied to every feature. [E012] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/spec-driven-development.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/spec-driven-development.md)

**Cost of delay** — [E] "The amount of an item's value that is lost by delaying its implementation by
a specified period of time." [E003] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Cumulative Flow Diagram** — [E] A chart plotting cumulative arrivals and departures from a system;
the slope of the hypotenuse is the average delivery rate. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Card, Conversation, Confirmation ("the 3 C's")** — Ron Jeffries' framing of a user story: the
written description, the discussion that builds understanding, and how the team knows it is
complete. [S127] → [06-requirements/user-stories.md](06-requirements/user-stories.md)

**CES (Customer Effort Score)** — "The percentage of respondents who select the highest 3 options on
a 1–7 ease scale," targeting the ease of a particular action. [S044] →
[09-metrics/product-metrics.md](09-metrics/product-metrics.md)

**Churn rate** — "The number of customers or subscribers who cut ties with your service or company,
divided by the total number of customers during a given time period." [S141] →
[09-metrics/product-metrics.md](09-metrics/product-metrics.md)

**Competitive analysis** — "A structured evaluation of competing and alternative products to
understand how they solve similar problems, where they perform well, and where gaps exist."
Distinguished from *competitor research* (raw information) and *market research* (the landscape).
[S025] → [03-market-intelligence/competitive-analysis.md](03-market-intelligence/competitive-analysis.md)

**Constraint** — Something "the eventual implementation can't require, be it a budgetary constraint
or a technical one." [S084] →
[10-risk/assumptions-and-constraints.md](10-risk/assumptions-and-constraints.md)

**Cost of delay** — A prioritization method based on "the ongoing costs that result from postponing
items on the backlog." [S066] →
[05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md)

**CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score)** — A one-question survey; "the percentage of respondents who
rated their satisfaction as a 4 or a 5 (out of 5)." [S044] →
[09-metrics/product-metrics.md](09-metrics/product-metrics.md)

## D

**Daily Scrum** — [E] A **15-minute** event for the Developers to "inspect progress toward the Sprint
Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary," held at the same time and place each working day.
[E001] → [07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Delivery Rate** — [E] Items delivered per unit time, measured at the delivery point. Called
**Throughput** when the segment measured does not end at the delivery point. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Developers (Scrum)** — [E] The Scrum Team members accountable for creating the Sprint Backlog plan,
adhering to the Definition of Done, adapting their plan daily, and holding each other accountable as
professionals. [E001] → [07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Dependency** — "Any known condition or item the product will rely on." [S084] More strictly: "the
factors your project relies on. You can't launch without them." [S048] →
[05-planning/dependencies.md](05-planning/dependencies.md)

**Discovery** — ⚠ **Two definitions in the corpus:**
- "A preliminary phase in the UX-design process that involves researching the problem space, framing
  the problem(s) to be solved, and gathering enough evidence and initial direction on what to do
  next." [S028]
- "The process of finding and validating product opportunities... figuring out what needs to be
  built." [S110]

→ [02-discovery/product-discovery.md](02-discovery/product-discovery.md)

**Double Diamond** — Four phases in two diamonds: Discover, Define (problem) → Develop, Deliver
(solution). Attributed to the UK Design Council, 2003. [S110], [S028] →
[02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md)

**Dual-track agile** — Parallel discovery and delivery workstreams. Attributed to a 2007 paper by
Desiree Sy. [S110] →
[02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md)

**DVF (Desirable, Viable, Feasible)** — IDEO's framework: a market willing to use it, a sound
business model, and the ability to execute. [S115] →
[12-design-and-ux/product-triad.md](12-design-and-ux/product-triad.md)

## E

**EARS (Easy Approach to Requirements Syntax)** — [E] A requirements notation created by Alistair
Mavin and colleagues at **Rolls-Royce, published 2009**. Generic form: *"While `<pre-condition>`,
when `<trigger>`, the `<system>` shall `<response>`."* Five patterns: ubiquitous, state-driven,
event-driven, optional feature, unwanted behaviour. [E014] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/spec-driven-development.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/spec-driven-development.md)

**Empiricism** — [E] Scrum's stated basis: "knowledge comes from experience and making decisions
based on what is observed." [E001] → [07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Eval (evaluation)** — [E] "A test for an AI system: give an AI an input, then apply grading logic to
its output to measure success." [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**Eval saturation** — [E] The point where an eval stops signalling improvement. [E011] flags **>80%**
on a benchmark as where signal-to-noise degrades. [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**End-of-life (EOL)** — The retirement of a product. Requires mapping "contractual and financial
obligations, data portability and migration assistance," with communication "most important of all."
[S118] →
[11-lifecycle-and-launch/decline-and-end-of-life.md](11-lifecycle-and-launch/decline-and-end-of-life.md)

**Epic** — ⚠ **Two models:**
- "An epic is **a large story**... As the epic gets closer to development, it should be split." [S127]
- "Epics are large work items broken down into a set of stories, and multiple epics comprise an
  initiative." [S126]

→ [06-requirements/epics-and-decomposition.md](06-requirements/epics-and-decomposition.md)

**Ethnographic research** — "Studying people in their natural environment rather than in a formal
research setting," to understand how they interact with their environment and the products in it.
[S029] → [02-discovery/ethnographic-research.md](02-discovery/ethnographic-research.md)

## F

**Feature factory** — A mature-product pathology: holding on to "the cadence of 'launch, launch,
launch'" and incentivising "delivery velocity, burndown, story points, and volume." [S042] →
[11-lifecycle-and-launch/managing-maturity.md](11-lifecycle-and-launch/managing-maturity.md)

**Functional requirements** — "Specify what the product should do from a user's perspective." [S085]
→ [06-requirements/requirements.md](06-requirements/requirements.md)

## G

**Go-to-market (GTM) strategy** — Covers market and customer definition, positioning and narrative,
pricing and packaging, channels and motion, success metrics, and operating model. [S034] →
[11-lifecycle-and-launch/go-to-market.md](11-lifecycle-and-launch/go-to-market.md)

## I

**Increment** — [E] "A concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal" — additive to prior
Increments, thoroughly verified and usable. **Work is not part of an Increment unless it meets the
Definition of Done.** [E001] → [07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — A company-level definition of the segment "where you have a real
shot at winning... where your product has a structural advantage." [S034] →
[11-lifecycle-and-launch/go-to-market.md](11-lifecycle-and-launch/go-to-market.md)

## J

**Job story** — "When [trigger], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]." Coined by Alan
Klement, 2014; originated at Intercom. [S053], [S052] →
[06-requirements/job-stories.md](06-requirements/job-stories.md)

**Jobs to be Done (JTBD)** — "People don't buy products; they hire them to get a job done."
Associated with Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta. [S054] →
[02-discovery/jobs-to-be-done.md](02-discovery/jobs-to-be-done.md)

## K

**Kanban (the Kanban Method)** — [E] ⚠ **Three distinct senses.** (1) **kanban**, lowercase: Japanese
for *sign / signal card*, used by Toyota from the 1960s. (2) **A kanban system**: a delivery flow
system limiting WIP by visual signals, with defined commitment and delivery points. (3) **The Kanban
Method**, capitalised: "a method for defining, managing, and improving services that deliver
knowledge work," applied to an existing process. **A board without WIP limits is none of these.**
[E003], [E004] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Kanban Litmus Test** — [E] Four prerequisite-ordered questions assessing how far an adoption has
gone: has **management behaviour**, the **customer interface**, the **customer contract**, and the
**service delivery business model** changed? **None asks about the board.** [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Kano model** — Sorts features into **basic** (expected), **performance** (scale with quality), and
**delighters** (unexpected). Attributed to Noriaki Kano. [S066], [S031] →
[05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md)

**Key result** — "Quantitative product metrics that show the progress you're making toward your
Objectives." 2–5 per objective. [S080] → [04-strategy/okrs.md](04-strategy/okrs.md)

**KPI vs OKR** — "OKRs are focused on a near-term objective, whereas KPIs are constant signals of
the health of the business." [S080] → [04-strategy/okrs.md](04-strategy/okrs.md)

## L

**Lead Time** — [E] The time an item takes between the commitment point and the delivery point. Use
**Time in Process (TiP)** for a narrower segment. [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Little's Law** — [E] In a non-trending flow system where selected items are delivered: **average
Lead Time = average WIP ÷ average Delivery Rate**. Its consequence: "in order to optimize the Lead
Time for work items, we must limit the Work in Progress." Original proof: Little (1961),
*Operations Research*. [E003] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

## M

**Market needs** — "The problems, desires, and gaps that exist in a target market." Three kinds:
functional, emotional, social. [S041] →
[03-market-intelligence/market-needs.md](03-market-intelligence/market-needs.md)

**MoSCoW** — Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have. Attributed to the DSDM Consortium.
[S066], [S031] →
[05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md)

**MRD (Market Requirements Document)** — "Describes customer demand, market opportunity, and a
business case." Distinct from a PRD, which "does not touch on market opportunity or revenue."
[S084] → [06-requirements/prd.md](06-requirements/prd.md)

**MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** — ⚠ **Barely defined in the corpus.** "The simplest version of your
product that can be released to early adopters... focus on the core features that address the
primary customer problem." [S110] The more useful framing: "A good MVP is **the least costly way to
prove out an idea** — the least costly way to achieve success or recognize failure." [S106] →
[02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md) — **priority gap**

## N

**Non-determinism (in acceptance)** — [E] The property that the same specification, given to an
agent twice, can produce two different implementations. It breaks the assumption that "passes" is a
property of the artifact rather than of a distribution. See **pass^k**. [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**NCT (Narrative, Commitment, Task)** — A goal-setting framework created by Ravi Mehta for teams
"consistently missing context." [S007] → [04-strategy/product-goals.md](04-strategy/product-goals.md)

**Non-functional requirements** — "Focus on the performance, reliability, security, and scalability
aspects... how the product performs." [S085] →
[06-requirements/requirements.md](06-requirements/requirements.md)

**North Star Metric** — ⚠ **Referenced by four sources, defined by none.** →
[09-metrics/north-star-metric.md](09-metrics/north-star-metric.md)

**NPS (Net Promoter Score)** — Likelihood of recommending, aggregated to a score from -100% to 100%.
"It should never be the only metric used to assess the usability of a system." [S044] →
[09-metrics/product-metrics.md](09-metrics/product-metrics.md)

## O

**Objective (OKR)** — "The outcomes, often qualitative, that you hope to achieve." Should be "a
reach goal... perhaps a little scary." [S080] → [04-strategy/okrs.md](04-strategy/okrs.md)

**One-way / two-way door** — Irreversible vs reversible decisions. Attributed to Jeff Bezos; "most
decisions are two-way doors [where] we should have a bias towards action." [S106] →
[04-strategy/strategic-thinking.md](04-strategy/strategic-thinking.md)

**Opportunity Solution Tree (OST)** — Outcome → opportunities → solutions → experiments. Attributed
to Teresa Torres. [S110] →
[02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md)

**Opportunity scoring** — ⚠ **[S066] gives two different formulas.** Identifies features of high
importance and low satisfaction. Attributed to Anthony Ulwick. →
[05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md) and
[99-reference/disputed-information.md](99-reference/disputed-information.md)

**Outcome vs output** — Outcome: "the desired results or changes you want to achieve." Output: "the
emphasis on producing deliverables, features, or completing tasks, regardless of whether they
directly contribute to meaningful results." [S080] → [04-strategy/okrs.md](04-strategy/okrs.md)

## P

**pass@k / pass^k** — [E] **pass@k** is the probability of **at least one** success in *k* attempts;
**pass^k** the probability that **all** *k* succeed. At 75% per trial, **pass^3 ≈ 42%**. [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**Probabilistic forecasting** — [E] Forecasting completion dates from **observed flow** rather than
summed estimates, using Monte Carlo simulation over historical lead times and delivery rates to
produce a distribution quoted at e.g. 50 / 85 / 95% confidence. [E003] →
[05-planning/estimation.md](05-planning/estimation.md)

**Product Goal** — [E] The Product Backlog's commitment: "a future state of the product which can
serve as a target for the Scrum Team to plan against." Must be fulfilled or abandoned before the next
is taken. [E001] → [07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Pull system** — [E] Work is "'pulled' into the system when other work is completed and capacity
becomes available, rather than 'pushed' into it when new work is demanded." Created by WIP-limit
policies. [E003] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**PESTLE** — Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental factors, used to
"surface both enablers and barriers." [S041] →
[03-market-intelligence/market-needs.md](03-market-intelligence/market-needs.md)

**Positioning** — "A strategic exercise that defines where your product or service fits in the
marketplace and why it is better than alternative solutions" [S083]; more sharply, "**planning for
how people in the market will think about your product**... the only product positioning that counts
is what your customers think." [S133] → [04-strategy/positioning.md](04-strategy/positioning.md)

**PRD (Product Requirements Document)** — "Defines the product to be built — outlining the product's
purpose, features, functionalities, and behavior." [S138] →
[06-requirements/prd.md](06-requirements/prd.md)

**Product-led growth (PLG)** — Growth driven "through the product itself using strategies like
freemium subscriptions, free trials, viral growth loops, and referral programs." [S098] →
[04-strategy/product-led-growth.md](04-strategy/product-led-growth.md)

**Product lifecycle** — Six stages in [S072]: product development, introduction, growth, maturity,
saturation, decline. ⚠ Other sources give four or five. Attributed to Theodore Levitt, HBR 1965.
→ [11-lifecycle-and-launch/product-lifecycle.md](11-lifecycle-and-launch/product-lifecycle.md)

**Product management** — "The practice of strategically driving the development, market launch, and
continual support and improvement of a company's products." [S118] →
[01-foundations/product-management.md](01-foundations/product-management.md)

**Product-market fit (PMF)** — ⚠ **Defined inconsistently.** The one operational test in the corpus:
"if 40% answer 'very disappointed'" to losing the product. Attributed to Sean Ellis. [S005] →
[04-strategy/product-market-fit.md](04-strategy/product-market-fit.md)

**Product owner** — A **Scrum role**; "works more closely with the development team to execute
against the goals that the product manager helps to define." [S078] →
[01-foundations/product-manager-vs-product-owner.md](01-foundations/product-manager-vs-product-owner.md)

**Product risk** — "The possibility that something about the product — what it does, how it's built,
or how it's received — could lead to product failure." [S087] →
[10-risk/product-risk.md](10-risk/product-risk.md)

**Product strategy** — "Outlines how a product will support company strategy and business goals...
It defines the 'why' behind the product." [S090] →
[04-strategy/product-strategy.md](04-strategy/product-strategy.md)

**Product triad / trio** — One designer, one product manager, one engineering lead, "working
together on one product as equal partners." Its purpose is "to **dissolve** siloed ownership."
[S115] → [12-design-and-ux/product-triad.md](12-design-and-ux/product-triad.md)

**Product value** — "The benefits customers get from using your product... **value is a subjective
concept, and customer perception lies at the heart of it.**" [S092] →
[04-strategy/product-value.md](04-strategy/product-value.md)

**Product vision** — Distinguished from a mission statement, which is "a slogan about their
purpose." A vision "serves as the North Star for the product organization." [S094] →
[04-strategy/product-vision.md](04-strategy/product-vision.md)

## R

**Regression eval** — [E] An evaluation verifying continued performance on previously mastered tasks;
target pass rate **near 100%**, so anything lower signals degradation. [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**Replenishment Meeting** — [E] The Kanban cadence for moving items **over the commitment point** into
the system and preparing future options. One of two cadences [E003] calls a baseline in nearly all
implementations. [E003] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**RARRA** — AARRR reordered retention-first. Proposed 2017 by Thomas Petit and Gabor Papp. [S021] →
[09-metrics/pirate-metrics.md](09-metrics/pirate-metrics.md)

**Release plan** — ⚠ **Contradictory definitions.** [S035]: includes "the timeframe when that work
will be completed." [S076]: "plot these milestones at a high level **without committing to a
particular timeframe**." →
[05-planning/release-planning.md](05-planning/release-planning.md)

**Residual risk** — What remains after mitigation: "as it is decreased, not removed, there can be
residual risk." [S065] → [10-risk/risk-response.md](10-risk/risk-response.md)

**RICE** — Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort; the product of the first three divided by the fourth.
Designed by Intercom. [S066], [S031] →
[05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md](05-planning/prioritization-frameworks.md)

**Risk** — PMI: "an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a **positive or negative**
effect on a project's objectives." [S100] →
[10-risk/risk-management.md](10-risk/risk-management.md)

**Risk register** — "A document compiling all known project risks," containing probability, impact
and triggers. The primary output of risk identification. [S100], [S043] →
[10-risk/risk-identification.md](10-risk/risk-identification.md)

**Roadmap (product)** — "A high-level visual summary that maps out the vision and direction of your
product offering over time... communicates the why and what." [S119] →
[05-planning/roadmaps.md](05-planning/roadmaps.md)

## S

**Scrum** — [E] "A lightweight framework that helps people, teams and organizations generate value
through adaptive solutions for complex problems." Its own boundary statement: "**While implementing
only parts of Scrum is possible, the result is not Scrum.**" [E001] →
[07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Scrum Master** — [E] Accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Guide, and for the team's
effectiveness. Serves the team, the Product Owner and the organization. [E001] →
[07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Service Request Manager / Service Delivery Manager** — [E] Kanban's two emergent roles. The first
understands customer needs and facilitates selection at the Replenishment Meeting — ⚠ [E003] gives
its **alternative names as "Product Manager, Product Owner, and Service Manager."** The second owns
flow. [E003] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Service levels** — [E] Four terms [E003] distinguishes and practice routinely conflates: **Service
Level Expectation** (what the customer expects) · **Capability** (what the system delivers) ·
**Agreement** (what is agreed) · **Fitness Threshold** (below which delivery is unacceptable). →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Spec-Driven Development (SDD)** — [E] Treating a written specification, not the code, as the source
of truth: humans author the spec, agents generate and verify code against it. "Define what and why
before deciding how to build it." [E012] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/spec-driven-development.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/spec-driven-development.md)

**Sprint** — [E] "The heartbeat of Scrum, where ideas are turned into value." Fixed length, **one
month or less**; contains all other Scrum events. [E001] →
[07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**Sprint Goal** — [E] The Sprint Backlog's commitment — "the single objective for the Sprint." **No
change may be made during the Sprint that endangers it**; scope around it may be renegotiated with
the Product Owner. [E001] → [07-execution/scrum.md](07-execution/scrum.md)

**STATIK** — [E] Systems Thinking Approach to Introducing Kanban: identify services, then per
service — fitness for purpose, sources of dissatisfaction, demand, capability, workflow, classes of
service, system design, socialisation. **Iterative, not sequential.** [E003] →
[07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Swiss Cheese model** — [E] Layered verification: "no single evaluation layer catches every issue.
With multiple methods combined, failures that slip through one layer are caught by another." [E011] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**SCAMPER** — Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse. Coined by
Bob Eberle from Alex Osborn's prompts in *Applied Imagination* (1953). [S018], [S101] →
[08-ideation/scamper.md](08-ideation/scamper.md)

**Scope creep** — "Work that's outside of the project's reach." [S048] →
[06-requirements/scope-definition.md](06-requirements/scope-definition.md)

**Service design** — "Planning and organizing a business's resources (people, props, and processes)
in order to (1) directly improve the employee's experience, and (2) indirectly, the customer's
experience." [S103] → [12-design-and-ux/service-design.md](12-design-and-ux/service-design.md)

**SEQ (Single Ease Question)** — A post-task rating "on a 7-point scale from very difficult to very
easy." [S044] → [02-discovery/surveys.md](02-discovery/surveys.md)

**Story mapping** — Seeing "how stories fit together across a user journey, workflow, product area,
or significant objective," revealing "missing steps, alternatives, assumptions, and release
options." [S127] → [06-requirements/epics-and-decomposition.md](06-requirements/epics-and-decomposition.md)

**Struggling moment** — "The moments when customers realize that their current solution is no longer
working." [S054] → [02-discovery/jobs-to-be-done.md](02-discovery/jobs-to-be-done.md)

**SUS (System Usability Scale)** — A 10-question post-test survey in use "since the 1980s," with
"established reliable benchmarks." [S044] →
[02-discovery/surveys.md](02-discovery/surveys.md)

**SWOT** — Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. Explained in the corpus only as a **risk
identification** tool. [S100] →
[10-risk/risk-identification.md](10-risk/risk-identification.md)

## T

**Task (vs story)** — "A story describes a useful outcome from the perspective of someone who
benefits from the work. A task describes work the team performs to deliver that outcome." [S127] →
[06-requirements/epics-and-decomposition.md](06-requirements/epics-and-decomposition.md)

**TTV (Time to Value)** — Named as a PLG characteristic to minimise; the interval before a user
perceives benefit. [S098] →
[04-strategy/product-led-growth.md](04-strategy/product-led-growth.md)

## U

**Use case** — Documents "both a user's goal and the functional requirements of the system,"
including preconditions, main flow, and alternate/exception flows. Attributed to Ivar Jacobson.
[S128] → [06-requirements/user-stories.md](06-requirements/user-stories.md)

**User story** — "A short description of desired functionality told from the perspective of someone
who wants or needs that functionality." The written sentence "is a reminder to have a conversation."
[S127] → [06-requirements/user-stories.md](06-requirements/user-stories.md)

## V

**Verification tax** — [E] ⚠ The review overhead on AI-generated work, named by [E009] as one of three
causes of the AI-adoption J-curve. Consistent with [E006]'s measurement that developers accepted
**under 44%** of AI generations and spent **~9%** of working time reviewing and cleaning AI output.
**The claim that it constitutes a bottleneck is an inference, not a finding.** →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evals-and-acceptance-for-agents.md)

**Value proposition** — "A concise statement that communicates... the benefits your product delivers
to users, and why they should choose you over the alternatives." [S005] →
[04-strategy/value-proposition.md](04-strategy/value-proposition.md)

**Value-Price-Cost** — Value is "the worth assigned to a product based on its benefits and utility";
price is "what you charge"; cost is "the costs involved in creating, maintaining, and improving."
[S092] → [04-strategy/product-value.md](04-strategy/product-value.md)

**Vanity metric** — A metric "just for boosting egos or press release fodder" rather than "tied to
customer satisfaction and business success." [S003] →
[09-metrics/product-metrics.md](09-metrics/product-metrics.md)

**Visiontype** — "A special form of prototype" used to make a product vision "visceral" by
dramatising the user experience. [S094] →
[04-strategy/product-vision.md](04-strategy/product-vision.md)

## W

**WIP limit (Work in Progress limit)** — [E] A displayed policy capping the items allowed in a stage.
Turns a push system into a pull system; per Little's Law, **the primary lever on lead time that does
not require adding capacity**. [E003] → [07-execution/kanban.md](07-execution/kanban.md)

**Working in small batches** — [E] Measured by lines of code per change, changes per release, and how
long a single task takes. [E008] found it **amplifies AI's positive influence on product performance**
and turns AI's neutral effect on friction beneficial — while slightly **reducing** AI's boost to
perceived individual effectiveness. [E008] →
[13-ai-agent-collaboration/evidence-on-ai-assisted-delivery.md](13-ai-agent-collaboration/evidence-on-ai-assisted-delivery.md)
