# Pirate Metrics — AARRR and RARRA

## Origin

[S021]: "**First presented by Dave McClure in his presentation 'Startup Metrics for Pirates' in
2007**, the AARRR method was originally meant for tracking product marketing and management and
**focused on acquisition.** However, **ten years later, Thomas Petit and Gabor Papp shifted the
steps of AARRR into RARRA — moving the focus to retention.**"

> **Verification status: UNVERIFIED.** The attributions and dates come from a single practitioner
> article; neither McClure's presentation nor Petit and Papp's work is in the corpus.

## AARRR — the five stages

[S021] defines each stage with a content-marketing example, so the definitions below are phrased in
that context:

| Stage | [S021] |
|---|---|
| **A — Acquisition** | "When someone **visits your website for the very first time** via a digital marketing channel" |
| **A — Activation** | "When that person has their **first positive user experience** with your site or marketing content" |
| **R — Retention** | "When that person **revisits** your website again in the future" |
| **R — Referral** | "When that person enjoys your site enough to **share it with others**, either publicly or privately" |
| **R — Revenue** | "When that person **makes a purchase and becomes a paying customer**" |

[S098] uses a six-stage variant — **AAARRR**, adding **Awareness** before Acquisition.

> The stage definitions are **channel-specific**. In a SaaS product, *activation* typically means
> reaching a first meaningful outcome inside the product, not a positive impression of a webpage.
> **The framework is a shape, not a fixed set of definitions** — each stage must be operationally
> defined for the specific product before it can be measured.

### Worked example
[S021] traces one path: a person searches Google for SaaS marketing strategies and lands on an
agency site — **acquired**. A week later they return via another article and subscribe —
**activated and retained**. They share content with colleagues — **referral**. Later, needing an
agency, they hire that one — **revenue**.

[S021] immediately qualifies it: "**this example is just one path of many that could define the
customer journey**... And it's also why AARRR may not be the best fit for every business."

## RARRA — the retention-first reordering

[S021]: "**RARRA is Dave McClure's AARRR model tweaked and updated:**"

| Stage | [S021] |
|---|---|
| **R — Retention** | "**Create incredible value to ensure everyone who visits your site is retained**" |
| **A — Activation** | "Make sure new visitors **see that value the very first time** they visit" |
| **R — Referral** | "Get them talking about and sharing your site/business with others" |
| **R — Revenue** | "Convert some people into customers **based on the value you provide**" |
| **A — Acquisition** | "**Use your paying customers to help you find more just like them**" |

### The argument for reordering

[S021]:

> "This update was first proposed in **2017 by Gabor Papp with regards to app businesses because
> the market has changed a lot**... when Apple first launched the App Store, **there were only 500
> apps to choose from. Now there are almost 9 billion worldwide** across multiple mobile app
> markets and countries.
>
> **And where there's competition, acquisition gets more expensive. So although it might have made
> sense to prioritise acquisition back when there was a younger industry, doing so now might not be
> as effective** for every business. Instead, **leading with a retention-first mindset may be a
> better option.**"

> **The reordering is not cosmetic — it changes what you optimise first.** AARRR treats the funnel
> as a pipeline to be filled at the top; RARRA treats it as a bucket that must hold water before
> filling is worthwhile. The argument is economic: when acquisition cost rises, retention
> determines whether acquisition pays back.
>
> ⚠ **The "9 billion apps" figure is implausible as stated** and is almost certainly an error or a
> conflation (global app *downloads*, perhaps). It is reproduced here because the source states it,
> but **it should not be cited.** Recorded in
> [../99-reference/disputed-information.md](../99-reference/disputed-information.md).

## Which model to use

[S021]'s answer is the useful part:

> "**The short answer is neither. While many businesses want to only adopt the model they see as
> most effective, they are essentially ignoring the fact that both models could suit your business
> at different times.**"

**It depends on** [S021]:
1. **The stage of your business**
2. **Your business goals**
3. **The industry you're in**

> "**A SaaS business may have a completely different funnel than an eCommerce business. And even
> two SaaS businesses may have customers who buy differently or completely different business goals
> depending on the stage they're in.**" [S021]

### How this connects to product work
The choice maps onto the **types of product work** in
[../01-foundations/pm-specializations.md](../01-foundations/pm-specializations.md):

| Situation | Likely emphasis |
|---|---|
| Pre-product-market-fit | Neither ordering matters much — the question is whether anyone retains at all. See [../04-strategy/product-market-fit.md](../04-strategy/product-market-fit.md) |
| Post-PMF, growth work | AARRR — "**capturing more of the existing market**" [S111] |
| Saturation, rising acquisition cost | RARRA — retention determines unit economics |

> This mapping is a **synthesis**, not a corpus claim.

## Relationship to product-led growth

[S098] uses AAARRR as the metric structure for **product-led growth**. Its PLG characteristics
include "minimize **TTV (Time to Value)**" and "**growth loops**" — both of which are activation
and referral mechanisms in pirate-metrics terms.

> Note the alignment: PLG's core claim is that the *product* does the acquiring, which is exactly
> RARRA's final stage — "use your paying customers to help you find more just like them." **A PLG
> strategy measured with an acquisition-first funnel is measuring the wrong end of its own
> mechanism.** See [../04-strategy/product-led-growth.md](../04-strategy/product-led-growth.md).

## Limitations

- **Single substantive source.** [S021] is a practitioner article by a former CPO turned agency
  owner, published by Mind the Product.
- **No primary sources** for McClure, Petit or Papp.
- **The 9-billion-apps figure is not credible** (see above).
- **No guidance on defining the stages operationally** for a given product — which is where the
  framework's real difficulty lies.
- **No treatment of how to instrument or calculate any stage**, cohort analysis, or funnel
  conversion mathematics.
- **Both AARRR and RARRA are funnel models**, and the corpus contains no engagement with the known
  critique of funnel thinking (that user journeys are not linear) — beyond [S021]'s own note that
  its example "is just one path of many."
