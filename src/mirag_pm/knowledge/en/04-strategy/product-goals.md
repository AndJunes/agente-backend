# Product Goals and Goal-Setting Frameworks

## What a product goal is

[S104] (Aha!): "A product goal is a **measurable, time-bound objective that supports your product
vision.** It defines **what you want to accomplish and how you will evaluate your success.**"

Its example: "you may set a product goal to **double your revenue in the next 12 months — with a
success metric of $100M** to consider this goal achieved."

### Cadence and ownership
[S104]: "Many organizations set new product goals **annually, bi-annually, or quarterly.**
Goal-setting is a **collaborative process between leadership and the product team**, guided by
higher-level business needs in addition to the overall product strategy."

### Where goals sit in the planning sequence
[S104]: "As part of this process, you will also define **product initiatives** — the broad themes
of work you are planning to achieve each goal. **After setting goals and initiatives, that is when
you can start product planning and building out a roadmap.**"

> Goals precede the roadmap. This matches [S090] in
> [product-strategy.md](product-strategy.md), where goals and initiatives are the "imperatives"
> layer of strategy, and [S119], where roadmap themes derive from strategic objectives.

### Why they matter
[S104]: "Without them, product teams struggle to make meaningful progress. Sure, you can make your
best guess... **You might get by with savvy gut decisions in the short-term. But if you want to
build towards lasting success... you need to establish concrete goals to work towards.**"

[S104] also notes goals are a precondition for clarity, not just measurement: "Setting product
goals... **requires you to have a full understanding of where you are headed and why.**"

## The central argument: there is no single right framework

[S007] (Reforge) is the corpus's most valuable source here precisely because it refuses to
recommend one framework:

> "We know that every team and company is different... **depending on the scope, resources, and
> even leadership style, the right framework for setting product goals will vary.** Maybe you want
> an approach that's better for small, remote teams. Or maybe you need to streamline differing
> goals from various teams, which requires a framework to communicate a top-down strategy.
>
> **Each of these five frameworks excels at different things.**"

It also names a common failure mode in framework adoption:

> "**Using something because it was assigned to you at a previous company is not the same as
> choosing to use a framework because it fits your needs.**"

## The five frameworks and when each fits

[S007]'s selection guide, stated up front:

| Framework | Use when… |
|---|---|
| **OKRs** | "You're trying to push a **large, established team to stretch themselves**" |
| **NCTs** | "You find the team seems to be **consistently missing context**" |
| **Dream Maps** | "You are trying to **keep a remote team connected**" |
| **MSPOTs** | "You need to **unite your team around a strategic vision but don't want to be prescriptive**" |
| **V2MOMs** | "You need **really strong top-down alignment**" |

### 2. NCTs — Narrative, Commitment, Task

**Attribution** [S007]: "**Ravi Mehta created the NCT goal setting framework** after observing that
many product teams lacked a clear path forward when working on ambiguous projects."

| Component | Definition [S007] |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** | "A **1-2 sentence qualitative description** of what the product team or pod wants to achieve in a quarter. A narrative describes **the outcome they want to achieve, and why it is important to the business**" |
| **Commitments** | "**3-5 objectively-measurable goals** that the team is committing to achieve by the end of the quarter. Commitments are **evidence that the team has made progress on the narrative**" |
| **Tasks** | "Identify the work that needs to be done to complete the commitments... **Tasks are more fluid than commitments. They are mainly a recommendation or a plan for work**" |

**Two types of commitment** [S007] — and this is the structural difference from OKRs:
1. **Quantitative metrics** — e.g. "increase onboarding completion rate by 10%"
2. **Deliverables** — e.g. "launch new onboarding flow by September 2019"

> NCTs **legitimise deliverable-based commitments**, which strict OKR practice treats as an
> output-measurement mistake (see the mistakes table in [okrs.md](okrs.md)). This is a real
> disagreement between the two frameworks, not a detail. NCTs accept that for ambiguous or
> greenfield work, "did we ship the thing" is sometimes the only honest near-term commitment.

**Why NCTs exist — the three OKR deficiencies they address** [S007]:
1. "Where OKRs don't provide enough context, **NCTs offer teams and leaders the opportunity to add
   important information that does not treat launches or product goals in isolation.**"
2. "While OKRs **don't guide product builders on what they must work on over what they should do
   eventually**, NCTs help teams **prioritize which commitments are critical versus
   aspirational.**"
3. "When OKRs **fail to define a path to delivery**, NCTs guide teams with **intermediary
   milestones.**"

**Worked example — Tripadvisor** [S007]:
- **Narrative:** "increase the share of traffic coming directly to the product, rather than SEO and
  SEM," based on the learning that "travelers who save hotels and other places for a trip are more
  likely to return directly to the platform." The plan: "increase awareness and usage of the *Save
  to Trip* feature."
- **Quantitative commitment:** "increase the number of unique savers by 10% by the end of the
  year's first quarter."
- **Deliverable commitment:** "launch the enhanced *Save to Trip* feature and CTAs to 25% of
  traffic by mid-February."
- **Tasks:** add an announcement modal for returning users; email the test group; add a prominent
  save CTA to the hotels list and detail pages; add a tooltip for users who have not used the
  feature; send price-update notifications for saved places.

> Note how the narrative encodes the **causal hypothesis** ("savers return directly"), which OKRs
> have no slot for. That is the "missing context" problem NCTs are designed to solve.

### 3. Dream Maps

**Attribution** [S007]: "Jonatan Littke, CEO of Squadformers."

**What it is** [S007]: "A Dream Map helps teams **choose a goal that they really care about
achieving, find the fastest path to get there, and tell a story about the journey.** The team
doesn't necessarily need to know the best path forward — **that's the whole point.**"

| Component | Definition [S007] |
|---|---|
| **The Dream** | "A big goal that everyone on the team **truly dreams of achieving**... something that inspires people to do their best work." Examples: "achieving a strategic partnership, finding product-market fit, raising Series A" |
| **Milestones** | "Intermediary steps the team will accomplish on their way... **Most milestones will be significant outcomes**" |
| **Signs** | "Indicate **progress toward** reaching the Dream... **Signs are outcome-based only. They don't demonstrate *how* a team will reach a goal, but show *how close* a team is.**" If the Dream is unambiguous (raise Series A) signs matter less; if it is fuzzy (find product-market fit) "regularly updated Signs will be necessary" |
| **Paths** | "Lines plotted on the map that represent various initiatives, like experiments, features, or projects. **Paths have different shapes depending on the level of uncertainty or level of effort**" |

**The distinctive property** [S007]: "a completed Dream Map will have **multiple paths** going from
the current position to the Dream. Some of these Paths will point directly toward the Dream...
**and other Paths will meander or fizzle out. All Paths demonstrate what the team or individual has
tried to do** in an effort to reach the Dream goal."

> The map **records failed attempts as first-class content.** No other framework in the corpus
> does this. It makes the artefact a learning history rather than only a plan.

**The decision rule built in** [S007]: "Leaders... can note if current paths to the Dream are
working well or need to be revised based on the Signs. **If Signs aren't changing, it might be time
to explore new Paths.**"

**Why remote teams** — Littke, quoted in [S007]: *"In a remote team, it's easy to get lost. It's
like, 'where are we and where are we going?' and 'where am I?' Dream Maps help teams maintain a
sense of momentum by answering these questions... And, **you literally can't get lost on a Dream
Map. Your avatar is right there, on a certain Path.**"*

**Nesting** [S007]: "companies can create a Dream Map at the executive level and connect it to
those at functional or individual levels... **you link a team or individual's Dream Map from a Path
on a higher-level dream map.**"

**A pointed criticism of OKRs** — Littke, quoted in [S007]: *"Other goal setting approaches, like
OKRs, are **corporate and full of jargon. OKRs don't really matter to people like dreams matter to
them.**"*

> This is a motivational claim from the framework's own creator and should be read as advocacy.

**Reported use** [S007]: Chris Zacharias, founder and CEO of Imgix, uses Dream Maps at executive
level. Quoted: *"The original Dream for Imgix was to be the processor of images — sort of similar
to Kodak back in the 1980s."* The company is remote-first; "Chris pulls up the Dream Map during
team syncs and they debate four or five paths toward the milestones."

## SMART goals

[S104] and [S048] both reference SMART as a goal-quality standard.

[S048]: "Generate **SMART goals — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound** —
that can later serve as goalposts."

[S104] refers to goals "outlined in SMART."

> The corpus gives **no guidance on applying SMART**, no critique of it, and no discussion of its
> known tension with the "stretch goal" property that [S080] requires of OKR objectives — an
> aspirational objective that is "**perhaps a little scary**" is not straightforwardly
> *achievable*. **This tension is unresolved in the corpus** and is worth noting when both
> standards are invoked at once.

## How to choose

Synthesising [S007]'s framing, the selection variables are:

| Variable | Which way it points |
|---|---|
| **Team maturity and size** | Large, established → OKRs |
| **Clarity of the path** | Ambiguous / greenfield → NCTs (context and intermediary milestones) |
| **Distribution** | Remote → Dream Maps (shared visual location) |
| **Desired degree of prescription** | Low → MSPOTs |
| **Direction of alignment** | Strong top-down → V2MOMs |

> **The meta-point is more durable than the list:** match the framework to the team's actual
> deficiency. A team that is aligned but unfocused needs something different from a team that is
> focused but lacks context.

## Limitations

- **Two of the five frameworks are not actually documented here** (MSPOTs, V2MOMs) due to source
  extraction loss. This document names them honestly rather than filling the gap from memory.
- **[S007] is a commercial education provider** and each section points to its own programs. Its
  contributors are named and credible, but the framework descriptions are second-hand summaries.
- **No evidence** that any of these frameworks improves outcomes. The Tripadvisor and Imgix
  examples are illustrative.
- **The Dream Map material is largely the creator's own advocacy**, including its criticism of
  OKRs.
- **No treatment of goal cascading across organizational levels**, goal-setting cadence trade-offs,
  or what to do when goals are missed.
- **SMART is invoked without explanation or critique** (see above).
