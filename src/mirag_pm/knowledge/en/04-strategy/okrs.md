# OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)

## Definition

[S080]: "OKR stands for Objectives and Key Results. **This goal-setting framework keeps both teams
and organizations focused on what matters while tracking progress toward an inspiring
destination.**"

Its structural role: "OKRs can be thought of as **the bridge between the high-level outcomes a
company seeks and the on-the-ground work that is being done to achieve this.**"

### The three parts

[S080] notes the framework actually has three components, though only two are in the acronym:

| Component | Definition [S080] |
|---|---|
| **Objectives** | "The outcomes, **often qualitative**, that you hope to achieve. They answer this question: **What value are you driving for the business or users?** The objective is usually something that can be accomplished within the quarter, or at most a year" |
| **Key Results** | "**Quantitative product metrics** that show the progress you're making toward your Objectives. There are generally **2-5 KRs per Objective.** They connect the operational actions your team takes on the day-to-day to measurable end results" |
| **Initiatives** | "Projects and tasks that help you achieve a Key Result. **They didn't make it into the 'OKR' acronym, but they're still vital to this framework**" |

[S007] gives a tighter definition of the pair: "**The objective:** what you hope to accomplish with
a given set of initiatives. **Key results:** numerically-based expressions of success or progress
towards an objective."

## Origin

[S080]: "One of the first people, **if not the very first**, to ever talk about OKRs was **Andy
Grove, CEO of Intel, back in the 70s. Google popularized OKRs in the early 2000s** and still uses
this framework today."

> **Verification status: UNVERIFIED.** This origin account is widely repeated and is
> attributed here to a training-provider blog with no citation. The primary sources (Grove's
> *High Output Management*, Doerr's *Measure What Matters*) are **not in the corpus.**

## How OKRs differ from adjacent artefacts

This is the corpus's most useful OKR content, because these confusions are common.

### The navigational analogy [S080]
> 📍 "LA is your destination, your **product vision**."
> 🛣️ "Your route is the **product strategy**."
> 🚸 "The road signs are your **OKRs** — they measure how far you've gone and how long you have to go (**and whether you need to speed things up** to make it to your next stop on time)."
> 🗺️ "Your trusty roadmap would be the **product roadmap** — a physical manifestation of the route."

### Explicit comparisons [S080]

| Versus | Distinction |
|---|---|
| **Product vision** | "Think of OKRs as **smaller, actionable versions of the product vision.** They are narrower in scope, **time-boxed (usually quarterly)**, and focus on what needs to be done in the short term to drive progress toward the product vision. Both OKRs and the product vision are 'aspirational'" |
| **Product strategy** | "Product strategy is the high-level plan that outlines *how* a product will achieve its goals... OKRs are used to **measure progress toward those goals.** ... **Strategy guides; OKRs quantify progress.**" |
| **Product roadmap** | "A product roadmap is an **output-focused** planning tool that outlines the deliverables needed to meet objectives. OKRs and product roadmaps complement each other: **the OKRs establish the key results needed to meet the objectives; the roadmap plans out the steps needed to meet KRs.**" And: "The ideal type of roadmap for planning out the path to achieving OKRs is an **outcome-based roadmap**" |
| **KPIs** | "**OKRs are focused on a near-term objective, whereas KPIs are constant signals of the health of the business.** KPIs are constantly being monitored, whereas KRs identify specific benchmarks that indicate progress toward a **time-boxed** objective" |

**The KPI/KR example** [S080]: "A **KPI** for a product like Netflix might be **session duration**
because it indicates the level of user engagement... On the other hand, if Netflix wants to
increase user engagement with original content, then a **key result** might be to **boost average
viewing hours per user by 15% for original content within the next two quarters.**"

> **The operative difference is time-boxing and intent.** A KPI is a standing instrument; a key
> result is a target attached to a specific objective for a specific period. The same metric can
> serve as both.

## Process: how to set OKRs

[S080]'s steps:

1. **Brainstorm objectives** — "aligned with product vision and high-level goals. Ask yourself:
   **Why should we prioritize this objective?** Narrow the list down to **3-5 objectives.**"
2. **Set key results** — "For each of the 3-5 objectives selected above, set **up to 5 KRs.** Make
   sure they are: **Measurable. Achievable. Aligned with the objective.**"
3. **Define initiatives** — "Each KR can have **1-5 associated initiatives.** Some initiatives may
   apply to more than one KR."
4. **Write the OKR statement** — format: *"I will (Objective) as measured by (Key Results)."*

### Who sets them
[S080]: "OKRs will typically be set **both at the team level and at the company level.** OKRs for a
Product organization or Product Team are set by a cross-functional group that delivers Product,
sometimes referred to as the '**triad**' or '**trio**,' which includes **Engineering, Product
Management and Product Design.**"

### The nature of a good objective
[S080]: "The Objective should be a '**reach goal**' that is inspirational, aspirational, something
worth getting excited about and **perhaps a little scary.** The Key Results should be measurable so
the team can track and report on progress towards the Objective in a robust way."

## What OKRs do well

[S007] gives four mechanisms, attributed to its named contributors:

| Mechanism | [S007] |
|---|---|
| **Create focused effort** | "By focusing on the top objectives and associated key results, product teams **have to make hard prioritization trade-offs upfront.** This also helps teams align on business and product **outcomes, rather than outputs**" |
| **Drive team empowerment** | "This alignment **empowers the team to make tradeoffs with less top-down involvement.** That way, when cross-functional peers approach the team with product requests, your teammates will be able to make decisions **quickly and with more confidence**" |
| **Create product intuition** | "By adopting an outcome-oriented approach to OKRs and implementing **a regular review process**, a team's learning loop can be improved and accelerated" |
| **Create leadership buy-in** | "If product managers in the organization are **all using OKRs**, it's easier to socialize your team's objectives and key results with executives" |

Quoted in [S007] — Sachin Rekhi, Founder & CEO of Notejoy: *"OKRs are one of the best goal setting
frameworks I've found for driving focus, alignment, accountability, and an outcome orientation."*

### When OKRs are the right choice
[S007] is unusually specific: OKRs "are the best way to set product goals when you're trying to
**push a large, established team to stretch itself to reach new heights.**"

## Common mistakes

[S080]'s table of failure modes and remedies is the most practical content here.

| Mistake | Best practice | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Inconsistent scoring framework** | "Pick the best framework for your needs and **stick with it.** Make sure everyone understands the framework and what success looks like" | Recommends **GYOR**: Green = 100% of KRs achieved; Yellow ≥ 70%; Orange ≥ 50% and < 70%; Red < 50% |
| **Setting and forgetting your OKRs** | "Your OKRs are only as valuable as your ability to use them to influence your team's day-to-day work. **Provide weekly updates** to keep them top of mind" | Updates should include: **% progress** toward each KR; **confidence** of completing each KR by quarter end (Completed / High / Medium / Low / Not on track); **key updates** — "notable progress made, exceptional circumstances, and roadblocks" |
| **Setting vanity metrics** | "**Connect your OKRs to your Product's North Star** to make sure there is a purpose behind your goals" | Example given: Basecamp's north star — "make project management more of a joy and less of a chore" |
| **Measuring outputs or feature completions instead of outcomes** | "**Outcomes > output!**" | "**Stop thinking:** We succeeded because we launched 3 new features. **Start thinking:** We succeeded because we reduced customer churn by 5%" |
| **Seeing OKRs as top-down with no input from individual contributors** | "Setting OKRs is a **collaborative process**" | Example: "Your data analyst and UX researcher share with you key touchpoints, helping you target critical moments and set realistic goals" |

> The **confidence rating** practice deserves emphasis: tracking not just progress but the team's
> confidence in reaching the target turns the OKR into a leading indicator rather than a lagging
> report.

## The outputs-to-outcomes shift

[S080] situates OKRs historically: "**Product people began shifting their focus from outputs to
outcomes in the early 2010s**, largely influenced by the rise of agile methodologies, lean startup
principles, and customer-centric approaches... **OKRs played a pivotal role in the shift.**"

Definitions given:
- **Outcome-focused:** "concentrating on the desired results or changes you want to achieve, such
  as solving customer problems or driving business impact."
- **Output-focused:** "places the emphasis on producing deliverables, features, or completing
  tasks, **regardless of whether they directly contribute to meaningful results or impact.**"

Quoted in [S080] — Ajay Swamy, Product Leader at Amazon: *"Being outcome-focused helps you become
more customer-focused."*

## A worked example

[S007] describes LinkedIn's 2014 Sales Navigator launch:

- **Objective:** "to launch the Sales Navigator product and define 'social selling'."
- **Key results:** "first an **output-oriented** metric to launch the product by July 31... Their
  **outcome-oriented** key results included **increasing weekly active users, increasing the sales
  team's conversion rate, and achieving a high NPS** with the product."
- **Reported result:** "LinkedIn launched the Sales Navigator product in July 2014 and saw
  **company revenue increase by 45% that year**, compared to the year before."

> **Evidence caution.** The 45% figure is attributed to no source, and the causal claim — "It
> helped that their product team used OKRs" — is hedged even by [S007]. Company-wide revenue
> growth cannot be attributed to one product team's goal-setting framework. **Treat this as an
> illustration of OKR structure, not as evidence that OKRs work.**
>
> Note also that the example mixes an **output KR** (launch by a date) with outcome KRs — which
> the same corpus elsewhere ([S080]) lists as a mistake. The tension is real and unaddressed.

## OKRs and roadmaps

[S062] recommends OKRs as the organising layer for outcome-based roadmaps: "Use a framework like
OKRs to organize objectives and outcomes. **This will make them easier to track and measure.**"

[S040] maps the concepts directly: "If you use objectives and key results, OKRs, then you can
**view the [three-month outcome] goal as an objective**." See
[../05-planning/outcome-based-roadmaps.md](../05-planning/outcome-based-roadmaps.md).

## Limitations and when OKRs may not fit

- **[S007] states the boundary directly:** "product teams working on **ambiguous initiatives** find
  that OKRs **don't provide enough context or guidance** to accomplish end goals." It proposes
  **NCTs** as the alternative — see [product-goals.md](product-goals.md).
- **[S007] lists three specific deficiencies of OKRs:**
  1. "Where OKRs don't provide enough context" for teams on new products;
  2. "**OKRs don't guide product builders on what they must work on over what they should do
     eventually**" — i.e. no distinction between critical and aspirational;
  3. "**OKRs fail to define a path to delivery**" — no intermediary milestones.
- **No evidence in the corpus** that OKRs improve outcomes. The LinkedIn example is anecdotal and
  its causal claim is unsupported.
- **No primary sources.** Grove and Doerr are absent.
- **No treatment of OKR cascading**, the debate over whether OKRs should be tied to compensation,
  or the known failure mode of sandbagging targets.
- Both principal sources are **commercial training providers** promoting OKR templates and
  courses.
