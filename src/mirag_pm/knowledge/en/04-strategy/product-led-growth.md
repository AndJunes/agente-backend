# Product-Led Growth and Growth Models

## Definition

[S098]: product-led growth drives acquisition, conversion and expansion "**through the product
itself** using strategies like **freemium subscriptions, free trials, viral growth loops, and
referral programs.**"

Its plain-language framing: "The basic idea of product-led growth is **show, don't tell.** Instead
of a massive billboard with a tantalizing photo of fried chicken, **let customers try a crispy
sample.**"

Quoted in [S098] — Rachel Obstler, EVP of Product at Heap: *"Compared to the sales-led model,
product-led growth is interesting because **it's incredibly scalable.**"*

## The four growth models compared

This is [S098]'s most useful content, because it frames PLG as **one choice among several** rather
than as a universally superior approach.

| Model | How growth happens [S098] | Characteristic motion |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing-led** | "Relies on a company's marketing efforts to drive revenue... often done through content marketing... **This content creates a growth loop that leverages social media to turn its audience into a loyal community**" | Broadcast a message |
| **Sales-led** | "Puts your sales team at the forefront... all of the leads gained across a company's efforts are funneled into the sales team, who are relied upon to close the deals." Named examples of success: **Salesforce, Oracle** | "A series of demos, dinners, and discussions with a sales rep" |
| **Customer-led** | "Starts **not with your funnel, but with your user**... Growth happens when teams build feedback loops — through interviews, in-product feedback, user behavior insights, and community engagement — and translate those insights into action... **co-creating value with your audience**" | Direct feedback and human connection |
| **Product-led** | The user "experiences its value firsthand through free trials, freemium models, or other self-service options" | The product itself |

### The distinguishing question
[S098] gives a clean test for sales-led vs product-led:

> "**The fundamental difference between sales-led and product-led growth is how a potential
> customer becomes familiar with and convinced to buy the product. If this is through a free
> trial, then we're talking product-led. If, on the other hand, it's through a series of demos,
> dinners, and discussions with a sales rep, then that's sales-led.**"

### Hybrid models
[S098] names two, and they are important because pure models are rare:

- **Product-led sales:** "starts with the product at the forefront, enabling users to experience
  its value firsthand through free trials, freemium models, or other self-service options. **Once
  users are actively engaged and see the product's benefits, a dedicated sales team steps in** to
  further nurture these leads."
- **Product-led marketing:** "bringing a product to market, promoting it, and driving its adoption
  among target users **that focuses on the product's unique value and solving user problems.**"

### A stated limitation of customer-led growth
[S098] is candid about the model closest to most product-management instincts:

> "While this approach can drive strong retention and organic advocacy, **it also risks being
> reactive or slow to scale if teams over-index on individual requests.**"

> This corroborates, from the growth side, the caution in
> [../05-planning/prioritization.md](../05-planning/prioritization.md) about treating customer
> requests as a roadmap.

## Characteristics of a PLG strategy

[S098]: "No PLG strategy is exactly the same, but most share several key characteristics":

- "Build **habit-forming** products that people love using"
- "**Encourage collaboration:** make the experience even better if friends/colleagues use the
  product"
- "**Seamless sign-up and onboarding** processes"
- "Product features promote **virality with built-in growth loops**"
- "**Minimize TTV (Time to Value)**"
- "**Freemium or free trial** model to deliver immediate value"
- "**Prioritize end-user experience**"
- "Iterate based on user feedback to increase satisfaction and drive organic growth"
- "**Personalized experience** to encourage customer loyalty and retention"

> Two of these are structural rather than cosmetic and deserve emphasis:
>
> **Time to Value (TTV)** — in a PLG model, the user decides before any human intervenes, so the
> interval between sign-up and first perceived benefit *is* the conversion mechanism. This is the
> only place the corpus defines TTV's strategic role.
>
> **Collaboration as a growth mechanism** — a product that works better with colleagues creates
> distribution from usage rather than from marketing spend. This is what "growth loop" means
> concretely.

## Where PLG sits organizationally

[S111] describes the **Growth PM** specialization as focused on "the customer's journey with a
product, through the lens of business metrics like acquisition, CAC, sign ups, free trial starts,
conversion/purchase rates, monetization, ARPU, and retention" — covering "sign up/registration,
onboarding, conversion/monetization, pricing, referrals/sharing, retention/transaction moments."

See [../01-foundations/pm-specializations.md](../01-foundations/pm-specializations.md), which also
notes that growth work "**creates and captures value by capturing more of the existing market**...
connecting customers to the value that already exists in the product, rather than creating new
value."

> That distinction matters for deciding whether a growth problem is actually a *value* problem.
> Growth work assumes value exists and is undiscovered; if the value is not there, growth tactics
> accelerate churn rather than revenue.

## Growth strategy types

[S011] (Product School) lists seven categories of business growth strategy. They operate at
company rather than product level:

1. **Revenue growth strategy**
2. **Market expansion strategy**
3. **Customer growth strategy**
4. **Operational growth strategy**
5. **Strategic partnerships and acquisitions**
6. **Sustainable growth strategy (ESG)**
7. **Customer retention and expansion strategy**

Quoted in [S011] — Stephen Hsu, CPO at Calendly, on The Product Podcast *(quote content did not
extract)*.

> **[S011] is thin on substance per category.** The list is recorded for completeness; the corpus
> does not develop any of the seven into usable guidance. Its own framing of customer-led vs
> sales-led vs product-led duplicates [S098], which is the better source.

## Metrics

[S098] references the **AAARRR / Pirate Metrics framework** (Awareness, Acquisition, Activation,
Retention, Referral, Revenue) as its metric structure for PLG. See
[../09-metrics/pirate-metrics.md](../09-metrics/pirate-metrics.md), which covers AARRR and the
RARRA variant and the argument between them.

> The AARRR-vs-RARRA argument is directly relevant here: [S021] contends that retention-first
> ordering suits products whose growth depends on existing users — which is precisely the PLG
> case.

## Limitations

- **Both sources are from the same training provider** (Product School), and [S098] is a promotion
  vehicle for its "Growth Metrics Cheat Sheet." The model comparison is substantive; much of the
  rest is not.
- **No evidence** that PLG outperforms other models, or under what conditions. The Salesforce and
  Oracle references are the only named examples and they are examples of *sales-led* success.
- **No treatment of when PLG does not work** — enterprise procurement, regulated industries,
  products requiring implementation, or high-consideration purchases. This is a significant
  omission given that [S098] presents PLG largely favourably.
- **Freemium vs free trial** is raised as a decision ("how to choose between offering free trials
  or freemium plan") but the section explaining the choice **did not extract.**
- **No treatment of growth loops mechanically** — the term appears repeatedly but is never
  defined or decomposed.
- **[S098] is dated January 2026** and [S011] July 2025; growth tactics are among the most
  time-sensitive material in this knowledge base. See
  [../99-reference/outdated-information.md](../99-reference/outdated-information.md).
