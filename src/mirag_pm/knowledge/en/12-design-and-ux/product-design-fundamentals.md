# Product Design Fundamentals for Product Managers

## Why a PM needs this

[S096] (Johanna Feick, Mind the Product) makes the case for fluency rather than skill:

> "**This is not about becoming a great designer, it's just about gaining a better understanding of
> how product design, its tools and processes work to improve your ability to collaborate.**"

Its practical recommendation: "**Try to build your own user flows, wireframes, mockups and
prototypes over a couple of weekends to deepen your experience and learnings.**"

> This mirrors the technical-fluency bar in
> [../07-execution/working-with-engineering.md](../07-execution/working-with-engineering.md): enough
> understanding to have the conversation, not enough to do the job.

## Design principles

[S096] pushes back on a common assumption:

> "**Design is often seen as a matter of personal taste, however there are certain principles that
> do to some degree objectively define what is considered good design and what isn't.**"

| Principle | [S096] |
|---|---|
| **Emphasis** | "Emphasis on **the right elements to produce clarity and lead the user to them**" |
| **Alignment and balance** | "To keep a clean look" |
| **Contrast** | "**To ensure readability and visual differentiation** between elements" |
| **Consistency** | "**To create a unified look and feel**" |
| **Proportion** | "To further ensure balance" |

> These give a PM a **vocabulary for design feedback that is not taste-based.** "This doesn't feel
> right" is not actionable; "the emphasis doesn't lead me to the primary action" is.

## The overlap with product management

[S096] observes something worth noting explicitly:

> "Gathering user insights is essential... **Interviews, surveys, focus groups and so on provide
> them with key insights. Sounds a bit like what product managers are doing? Well, the overlap
> between product design and product management might be bigger than you think! Just like product
> managers are thinking about personas and user journeys, product designers have to follow a
> similar train of thought to understand what design resonates with their target group.**"

> This overlap is precisely what [S064] measured and found contested — PMs and UXers disagreed most
> about who should own discovery, ideation and early design. See
> [../01-foundations/pm-and-ux-collaboration.md](../01-foundations/pm-and-ux-collaboration.md).
> [S096] treats the overlap as a collaboration opportunity; [S064]'s data shows it is also a
> reliable source of friction. **Both are true.**

"Lastly, **usability testing allows us to test prototypes with real users to validate design
decisions.**" See [../02-discovery/usability-testing.md](../02-discovery/usability-testing.md).

## A design process

[S096] sketches a six-step sequence:

1. **Understand** the user, market, competitors and trends
2. **Define the problem, prioritize problems**
3. **Brainstorm solutions**
4. **Prioritize solutions / identify USP**
5. **Design** — "info. architecture, user flows, wireframes, mockups" [and prototypes]
6. **Get feedback and iterate**

> Note steps 1–2: the design process begins in the **problem space**, not with visual work. This is
> the same shape as the Double Diamond — see
> [../02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md](../02-discovery/discovery-frameworks.md) — and the same
> ordering [S136] requires for brainstorming ("haven't done basic research → skip brainstorming").

## Wireframes, mockups and prototypes

[S085] gives the clearest distinction in the corpus:

| Artefact | Definition [S085] |
|---|---|
| **Wireframe** | "Provide a **skeletal outline of the user interface, focusing on layout and functionality without including visual design details**" |
| **Mockup** | "**More detailed representations that include visual design elements such as colors, typography, and branding**" |
| **Prototype** | [S085] groups "interactive prototypes" with these artefacts; [S122] defines prototyping as "creating **lightweight, low-fidelity representations of a product idea** — paper prototypes, clickable wireframes, or interactive mockups" |

**Their shared purpose** [S085]: "**give stakeholders a clear visualization of the end product and
set design expectations**... facilitate communication between product managers, designers, and
developers, enabling alignment on the visual and functional aspects."

**A caution on where they belong** [S084], on PRDs: "**This is not the place for pixel-perfect
mockups or wireframes that map out every possible scenario; instead, it can be used to describe the
overall user workflow.**" See [../06-requirements/prd.md](../06-requirements/prd.md).

> [S086] separately names *"Wireframe vs. mockup vs. prototype"* as a topic it covers — **that
> content is not in the corpus.** The distinctions above are assembled from scattered mentions.

## Prototyping as a PM skill

[S122] treats prototyping as one of five PM hard skills:

> "**Prototyping is an essential tool for validating ideas, testing hypotheses, and iterating on
> product concepts quickly and cost-effectively.** ... Product managers must know how to **choose
> the right prototyping tools and techniques for the task at hand**... They must also **design
> experiments that yield actionable insights, define success metrics, and iterate based on user
> feedback.**"

See [../01-foundations/product-manager-skills.md](../01-foundations/product-manager-skills.md) and
[../02-discovery/hypotheses-and-assumptions.md](../02-discovery/hypotheses-and-assumptions.md).

## Collaborating with designers

[S096] devotes a section to "Collaborating with the design team" whose content did not extract.
What the corpus supplies instead:

- **Engage design before engineering.** [S113]: "**It is a good idea to engage design before
  approaching engineering because their feedback might affect the project's technical scope.**"
- **Don't present someone else's design.** [S064]: "**it's not a good idea to have someone else
  other than the designer pitch a design — the presenter may not properly understand the reasoning
  behind each decision and may not accurately convey feedback back to the designer.**"
- **Make personas a team effort.** [S138]: "Make developing and using customer personas a team
  effort. **Each team member has unique perspectives and insights.**"
- **Use visuals in requirements.** [S046]: text-only feature descriptions produced work that "missed
  key elements, requiring several costly and time-consuming revisions." See
  [../07-execution/working-with-engineering.md](../07-execution/working-with-engineering.md).

## Limitations

- **[S096] is a community blog post** and several sections — UI/UX essentials in depth, collaborating
  with the design team, and the actionable-tips detail — did not extract. It closes by recommending
  an external course, which is excluded as promotional.
- **[S123] (Figma, *UX for Product Managers*) contributed almost nothing** — the captured document
  is predominantly navigation. Its only extracted substance is a reference to "Customer satisfaction
  and NPS."
- **[S069] (IxDF, *Product Design and UX Design Roles*) is a stub** — only its table of contents
  extracted, so **the product-designer vs UX-designer distinction is not in this knowledge base.**
  See [../99-reference/excluded-content.md](../99-reference/excluded-content.md).
- **No treatment of design systems, accessibility, information architecture, interaction design, or
  visual design** beyond the five principles above.
- **No design critique method** — how a PM should give design feedback productively.
