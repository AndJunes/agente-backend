# Go-to-Market Strategy

## What GTM covers

[S034] gives the most structured account in the corpus — six components.

### 1. Market and customer definition
> "**Every strong GTM starts with a very specific 'who' and a very specific 'why now'.** Instead of
> going 'after enterprises' or 'SMBs', **you are choosing a segment where you have a real shot at
> winning. You need a clear industry, company size, region, or business or use case where your
> product has a structural advantage.**" [S034]

Within that: "**Ideal customer profiles (ICPs)** at the company level" and personas/segments.

> "**Structural advantage**" is the operative phrase — not merely a segment you could serve, but
> one where something about your position makes you likely to win.

### 2. Positioning, value proposition, and narrative
> "Positioning is **the slot you claim in your customer's mental market map: the category you play
> in, the alternatives you are compared against, and the reason you are the better choice for this
> specific context.**" [S034]

> This is a sharper definition of positioning than the one in
> [../04-strategy/positioning.md](../04-strategy/positioning.md) — it names three components
> (category, alternatives, reason) and is consistent with [S133]'s "the only positioning that
> counts is what your customers think."

"The value proposition turns that into something concrete: **What outcomes do you improve?**"
See [../04-strategy/value-proposition.md](../04-strategy/value-proposition.md).

### 3. Pricing, packaging, and commercial model
[S034]:
- "**What you charge for** (users, usage, seats, credits, features)"
- "**How you bundle capabilities into tiers or plans**"
- "**Whether you lead with free, trial, freemium, or paid-only**"

> ⚠ **This is the corpus's most substantive treatment of pricing anywhere**, and it amounts to
> three bullet points. Pricing strategy is a **corpus-wide gap** — see
> [RESEARCH_BACKLOG.md](../RESEARCH_BACKLOG.md).

[S124] adds a pricing checklist for launch: "**Business model defined** (freemium, SaaS,
advertising-driven, one-time sale) · Agreement on the **cost of goods sold** · **Competitor pricing
understood** · Pricing including any **product tiers/options** · **Wholesale vs. retail pricing**."

### 4. Channels and GTM motion
[S034]: "how you will actually reach and convert the people you care about."

| Motion | [S034] |
|---|---|
| **Sales-led** | "Outbound, demos, multi-stakeholder deals" |
| **Product-led** | "**Self-serve signup, in-product upgrades, usage-driven expansion**" |

See [../04-strategy/product-led-growth.md](../04-strategy/product-led-growth.md), which covers the
four growth models and their hybrids in more depth.

### 5. Revenue and success metrics
[S034] groups them by funnel position:

| Group | Metrics named |
|---|---|
| **Acquisition** | "Signups, demo requests, qualified pipeline, **CAC**" |
| **Activation and adoption** | "**Time-to-value**, activation rate, feature adoption" |
| **Revenue** | "**ARR added, ACV, win rate, conversion from trial or POC**" |

> This is the corpus's most specific metric list. See
> [../09-metrics/product-metrics.md](../09-metrics/product-metrics.md), where most of these terms
> appear as vocabulary without definitions.

### 6. Operating model and enablement
[S034]'s questions — these address the coordination problem that sinks most launches:
- "**Who owns the GTM strategy, and who owns execution day to day?**"
- "**How do product, marketing, sales, and customer success make decisions together?**"
- "**What enablement do sales and success teams need to tell the story?**"

> Component 6 is the one product managers most often treat as someone else's problem. [S119] makes
> the same point from the roadmap side: sales and marketing "are also critical stakeholders to
> share the product roadmap with **to plan out their go-to-market activities for key releases.**"

## GTM starts from the problem

[S033] (Pragmatic Institute) organises GTM differently, and its ordering is worth keeping:

1. **Start with the problem**
2. **Understand the buyer**
3. **Define the value clearly**

Its framing — "**What Most Teams Get Wrong About Go-to-Market Strategy**" — positions GTM as a
product-thinking exercise rather than a marketing campaign.

> This matters because GTM is frequently treated as post-build packaging. [S072] places it in the
> **introduction stage** of the lifecycle, where the team is still "gauging how well they assessed
> product-market fit." See [product-lifecycle.md](product-lifecycle.md).

## When product management owns GTM

[S037] addresses solo PMs and lean teams without a product marketing function:

- "**What GTM Actually Involves (from PMM's lens)**"
- "**Practical Tactics for Solo PMs**"
- "**How to Collaborate Without a PMM**"

> The sections' contents did not extract. The article's existence is recorded because the
> situation it addresses — **a PM absorbing GTM responsibility with no PMM counterpart** — is
> common and otherwise unrepresented in the corpus.

## Spanish-language sources

Two corpus sources cover GTM in Spanish:
- **[S014]** (Asana) — *9 pasos para diseñar una estrategia de Go-to-Market*, a nine-step process
  covering market identification, message, pricing and channels.
- **[S143]** — *¿Qué es una estrategia de comercialización? Guía completa de GTM*, whose components
  are: "**1. Definición del mercado** (¿A qué [mercado te diriges]?) · **2. Los clientes** (¿Quién
  es el público [objetivo]?) · **3. Modelo de distribución** (¿Cómo [llegas a ellos]?) ·
  **4. Mensaje y posicionamiento** del [producto] · **5. Precio** (¿Cuánto debería costar?)"

> These overlap substantially with [S034]'s components and add no distinct content. They are
> recorded in [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md) for traceability. [S143] also distinguishes GTM strategy
> from **account-based marketing** and contrasts a launch objective ("**Launching a specific**
> [product]") with a brand-building objective ("**Building and** [sustaining brand]") — a
> distinction worth noting: *a GTM strategy for a launch is not the same as an ongoing demand
> strategy.*

## GTM versus adjacent strategies

[S090] names two distinctions it treats but whose content did not extract:
- **Product strategy vs go-to-market strategy**
- **Product strategy vs marketing strategy**

What the corpus supports:

| | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Product strategy** | What we build and why — direction over the product's life [S090] |
| **Go-to-market strategy** | How a specific offering reaches and converts a defined segment [S034] |
| **Launch plan** | The time-bound execution of a GTM for one release — see [product-launch.md](product-launch.md) |

## Limitations

- **[S034] is the only structured source**, and it is a training provider's guide (Product School,
  updated Jan 2026) with heavy template promotion.
- **[S033] and [S037] are largely unextracted**; only their section structure survives.
- **No evidence** for any GTM recommendation.
- **Pricing is named as a core component and never taught** (see above).
- **No treatment of GTM for internal products, platforms, or free products.**
- **No treatment of launch sequencing across segments or geographies.**
- **No treatment of how GTM differs by company stage** — a startup's first GTM and an enterprise's
  hundredth are not the same exercise.
- GTM material is **time-sensitive**: channel effectiveness and motion norms change. [S034] is
  dated January 2026.
